Conclusion

The regression models achieved a maximum accuracy of approximately 70%, suggesting that the available features provide moderate predictive power for mental health outcomes. The results indicate that the relationships among variables may not be strong enough to fully explain the complexity of mental health patterns, which likely limited the model’s predictive performance.

The analysis highlights that mental health is influenced by a combination of demographic, behavioral, and environmental factors that are difficult to capture using traditional regression models alone. Future research could explore unsupervised learning techniques such as Clustering, Principal Component Analysis (PCA), and Self-Organizing Maps (SOM) to identify hidden structures and latent patterns within the data.

Overall, this project demonstrates the value of combining exploratory data analysis, statistical methods, and machine learning techniques to gain deeper insights into mental health trends and support data-driven decision-making.

This project highlights the importance of leveraging data science to better understand complex human-centered challenges and transform raw data into actionable insights.
